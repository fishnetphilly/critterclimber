This project started after a video showed 20 raccoons trapped inside a single dumpster.

Not one or two. Twenty. And we couldn't stop thinking: what's the fix? Turns out nobody had one.

The problem

Smooth walls. No way out.

Animals smell food, fall in, and can't get back out. Dumpster walls weren't designed for escape. Nothing to grip, no footholds, no way up.

This happens constantly to raccoons, opossums, squirrels, and cats. And as far as we can tell, there's no standard fix. No regulation. Nothing.

What it looks like when they have a way out.

A dumpster in Fishtown, Philadelphia. Smooth walls. No way out.

The solution

One piece. Clamps on in seconds.

A curved, textured panel that clamps over the dumpster lip from the outside. No tools. No drilling. No climbing inside. The slight curve keeps the bottom pressed against the interior wall — close enough to a ramp that any animal can use it.

When the garbage truck empties the dumpster, the clamps stay on. When it rights itself, the panel falls back into position. Self-resetting, every time, forever.
